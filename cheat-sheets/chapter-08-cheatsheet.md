# Chapter 8 Cheat Sheet: Cable Support & Weight Management

## NEMA VE 1 Tray Load/Span Designation
| Format | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Number (e.g. 8, 12, 16, 20) | Support span in feet |
| Letter (A, B, C) | Load class — increasing working-load capacity from A → C |
| Example: 12B | 12-ft support spacing at Class B working load |

**Rule:** Always evaluate load at EACH support point, not just total tray/run capacity. A single overloaded point can fail even if the overall system rating is fine.

## Overhead Support System Comparison
| System | Best For | Watch Out |
|---|---|---|
| Ladder rack | High-density, heavy bundles, needs visual/physical access | Requires trapeze/hanger structure sized for load + growth margin |
| Basket (wire mesh) tray | Lighter, mixed low-voltage runs, flexible routing | Lower load capacity than ladder rack |
| Solid-bottom enclosed tray | Physical protection, EMI shielding | Traps heat, needs lid removal for audit |
| Trapeze support | Structural mounting method for tray/ladder rack from ceiling deck | Must be rated for full designed load, not just current fill |

## Cable Support Design Checklist
- Size support system for calculated load **plus growth margin**, not just day-one fill.
- Distribute weight evenly — check **each support point**, not only aggregate rating.
- Mount overhead tray with clearance above cabinet tops for technician access and airflow.
- Separate power and low-voltage/fiber pathways per design/code requirements.
- Keep ladder rack/tray visually accessible for audits without disassembly.

## Service Loops (Slack Management)
- Purpose: allow future re-termination/moves and relieve mechanical strain at connectors/ports.
- Never pull cabling taut directly into a port — always leave slack near termination points.
- Loops should follow manufacturer minimum bend radius; excess unmanaged slack should still be neatly dressed, not left loose in the tray.

## Team Orientation Plan — Core Elements (define BEFORE work starts)
| Element | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Zone assignments | Prevent overlap/gaps in coverage across floors/rooms |
| Labeling convention | Consistent identification for audits and troubleshooting |
| Pull sequence | Efficient, conflict-free order of operations |
| Safety checkpoints | Verify load ratings, ladder/lift safety, PPE before work zones open |

**Common exam trap:** Assuming certified technicians don't need job-specific orientation — project-specific zone maps, labeling schemes, and pull order are still required regardless of certification level.

**Common exam trap:** Confusing 'tray total rated capacity is fine' with 'every support point is fine' — always check individual point loading, especially at merge/riser points.
