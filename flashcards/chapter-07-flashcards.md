## Chapter 7: Routing, Bend Radius, and Professional Dressing — Flashcards

| # | Front (Question) | Back (Answer) |
|---|-----------------|---------------|
| 1 | What is the exam-tested minimum bend radius for high-density cabling in NVL72-class racks? | ~30mm. Violating this causes attenuation (fiber) or signal degradation (copper) and risks physical damage. Exam tip: any scenario showing a tight loop below this threshold = violation. |
| 2 | What routing strategy is used to distribute cable load across an NVL72 rack's vertical channels? | 50/50 left-right routing strategy — split cable runs evenly between left and right channels to prevent overfill, airflow blockage, and forced tight bends. |
| 3 | Zip ties vs hook-and-loop straps for bundling fiber cables — which is correct for high-density dressing? | Hook-and-loop (Velcro) straps. They distribute pressure evenly and avoid the point-load crushing that zip ties can inflict on fiber cores. |
| 4 | What physical effect occurs when fiber is bent below its minimum bend radius? | Micro-bend and macro-bend attenuation — increased signal loss, possible link failure, and risk of permanent fiber fracture over time. |
| 5 | What does TIA-606 govern in rack cabling? | Administrative labeling and documentation standards — every cable label must trace a unique identifier to its origin and destination endpoints. |
| 6 | Why is a service loop (slack) left at cable termination points? | To allow future re-termination or maintenance without full cable replacement — without creating excess bulk that would violate bend-radius limits in the tray. |
| 7 | What happens if all cables in an NVL72 rack are routed down only one side (violating 50/50 strategy)? | Single-channel overfill — airflow obstruction, thermal hotspots, and forced tight bends at tray exits, risking bend-radius violations. |
| 8 | What is the risk of using zip ties tightened firmly around a fiber trunk bundle? | Point-load compression on the fiber jacket/core, potentially causing micro-fractures and signal attenuation — hook-and-loop is preferred instead. |
| 9 | What is the primary purpose of color-coding cables in a high-density rack? | Rapid visual identification of cable function/path (management vs. data vs. inter-rack) without physically tracing each run. |
| 10 | Which two cable types are BOTH subject to minimum bend-radius constraints? | Fiber AND copper. Common exam trap: assuming only fiber is bend-sensitive — copper conductors also suffer impedance/signal issues when overbent. |
| 11 | What minimum data must a TIA-606-compliant cable label include? | A unique identifier linking the cable's origin endpoint to its destination endpoint — enabling traceability without physical tracing. |
| 12 | Fiber trunk bundling: soft ties vs metal clamps — which is correct and why? | Soft hook-and-loop ties — metal clamps are too rigid and risk crushing/cracking fiber cores under concentrated pressure. |
