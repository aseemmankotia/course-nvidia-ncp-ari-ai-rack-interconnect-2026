## Chapter 8: Cable Support and Weight Management — Flashcards

| # | Front (Question) | Back (Answer) |
|---|-----------------|---------------|
| 1 | NEMA VE 1 tray designation '16C' — what does it mean? | 16-foot support span, Class C working load rating. Number = span in feet, letter = load class (A/B/C, increasing capacity). |
| 2 | Why must support points be evaluated individually, not just total tray capacity? | A single overloaded support point can deflect or fail (bracket/point-load failure) even if the tray's aggregate rated capacity is not exceeded. |
| 3 | Ladder rack vs. solid-bottom enclosed tray — key tradeoff? | Ladder rack: open airflow, easy visual audit, easy mid-span access. Solid-bottom tray: better physical protection but traps heat and needs cover removal to inspect. |
| 4 | Basket (wire mesh) tray vs. ladder rack — main difference in use case? | Basket tray suits lighter, mixed low-voltage runs with good flexibility; ladder rack suits heavier, higher-density loads with stronger rung-based support. |
| 5 | What does a trapeze support system consist of? | Threaded rod hangers from the structural ceiling deck supporting a horizontal channel (the 'trapeze') that carries cable tray or ladder rack. |
| 6 | Service loop — primary function? | Provides slack for future re-termination/moves and relieves mechanical tension/strain at connector and port terminations. |
| 7 | Overhead cable tray placement relative to cabinet tops — best practice? | Mount tray with clearance above cabinet tops so technicians can access rack tops and containment/airflow is not obstructed. |
| 8 | First step in a team orientation plan for a large cabling workflow? | Define zone assignments, labeling conventions, and pull sequence BEFORE any cable pulling begins. |
| 9 | What is the risk of pulling fiber patch cords taut with no slack into a transceiver port? | Mechanical strain transmits directly to the connector/port, risking micro-bend attenuation or physical port/connector damage during future moves. |
| 10 | Weight distribution fix when one support point on a tray run carries excess load from a riser merge point? | Redistribute cable bundles across points or add an intermediate support near the merge — do not rely on total tray rating alone. |
| 11 | When should intermediate cable tray supports be added? | When a single support point's calculated load exceeds its per-point rating, regardless of whether the tray's total rated capacity is still within limits. |
| 12 | Why avoid zip-tying cable bundles directly to containment frame structures? | Containment frames are not rated for cable weight; this creates uncontrolled point-load stress and is not proper weight-distribution practice. |
