## Chapter 12: Full Exam Simulation and Time-Management Strategy — Flashcards

| # | Front (Question) | Back (Answer) |
|---|-----------------|---------------|
| 1 | NCP-ARI exam: total questions and total time? | 70 questions, 120 minutes. Exam tip: this yields ~1.7 min/question raw average, but plan for 1.5 min/question to bank a review buffer. |
| 2 | If you average 1.5 min/question across all 70 questions, how much buffer time remains? | 15 minutes (70 × 1.5 = 105 min; 120 − 105 = 15 min). Use this buffer exclusively for flagged questions. |
| 3 | Flag-and-return strategy: when should you flag a question? | When you can make a confident best guess but aren't fully certain — answer it, flag it, move on. Never leave a question blank while pondering. |
| 4 | How should limited review time be allocated across flagged questions from multiple domains? | Proportional to domain weight — review flags in the heaviest-weighted domains first, since fixing an error there has the largest score impact. |
| 5 | Common exam trap pattern in scenario questions? | An option that is 'technically correct but requires an extra unstated step' is usually a distractor. Use as a tiebreaker, not a sole deciding rule. |
| 6 | Certiverse remote proctoring: what must the webcam scan show before starting? | A full view of the desk and surrounding room confirming no notes, phones, secondary monitors, or unauthorized materials are present. |
| 7 | Is a second monitor allowed during a locked-down Certiverse exam session? | No — even a second monitor turned off or facing away is typically not permitted; single-monitor mode is standard for remote-proctored certification exams. |
| 8 | Are personal scratch notes from home allowed during the exam? | No — only proctor-approved materials or the exam's built-in whiteboard/notes tool (if provided) may be used. |
| 9 | What should your final pre-exam study session cover? | A high-level pass across all seven official domains, with extra time weighted toward the heaviest-weighted domains — not a single deep dive into one weak domain. |
| 10 | How should time-management pacing be checked mid-exam? | Periodically compare questions completed vs. elapsed time against the 1.5 min/question target; if behind pace, flag more aggressively instead of slowing down further. |
| 11 | Why prioritize review of heavily-weighted domains over lightly-weighted ones with limited time? | Expected score impact scales with domain weight — fixing an answer in a heavy domain yields more score benefit per minute spent than in a light domain. |
| 12 | What's the risk of re-reading every question twice as a blanket strategy? | It consumes the review buffer meant for flagged questions and provides no guaranteed accuracy improvement — targeted flag-and-return review is more time-efficient. |
